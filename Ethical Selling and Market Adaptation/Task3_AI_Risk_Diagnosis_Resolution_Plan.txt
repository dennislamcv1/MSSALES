3A. RISK SCENARIO DESCRIPTION (120–150 words)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Unauthorized Disclosure of Client Vulnerability Data
in a Copilot-Generated Proposal

During a routine proposal drafting session, a TechSolutions
sales representative used Microsoft Copilot to accelerate the
creation of a cybersecurity assessment proposal for a prospective
client in the healthcare sector. To personalize the document, the
rep input detailed vulnerability findings from a previously
completed internal audit for an existing client — HealthBridge
Medical Group — as reference context in the Copilot prompt. The
resulting proposal inadvertently incorporated specific HealthBridge
network topology details and unresolved security gaps into the new
client's document, including language closely mirroring the
confidential audit report.

This was problematic for three reasons: it constituted an
unauthorized disclosure of HealthBridge's confidential security
posture; it potentially violated HIPAA's data handling obligations;
and it exposed a critical operational vulnerability belonging to a
live client to an unrelated third party. Had this proposal reached
the prospect without review, TechSolutions would have faced
immediate breach-of-contract liability, HIPAA notification
obligations, and catastrophic reputational damage — particularly
in a sector where data trust is the foundation of every
client relationship.

3B. DIAGNOSTIC BREAKDOWN (6 Items)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ITEM 1 — Detection Method
• How It Was Caught: A second-stage human reviewer — following
  TechSolutions' two-person proposal review protocol established
  in Task 1 — recognized a client name and network terminology
  that did not belong to the prospect being targeted. The
  reviewer flagged the document before it left internal systems.
• Key Takeaway: This incident confirms that the human review
  gate is not procedural overhead — it is an active line of
  defense. Without it, the error would have reached a third
  party undetected.

ITEM 2 — Suspected Root Cause
• Primary Cause: The sales representative lacked a clear
  understanding of context boundary risks in AI prompting —
  specifically, that confidential data pasted into a Copilot
  session can be recalled, recombined, and surfaced in
  unintended outputs, especially within the same session or
  connected workspace environment.
• Contributing Cause: No prompt construction guidelines existed
  to prohibit the use of identified client data as AI input
  context, leaving individual judgment as the only safeguard —
  an insufficient control for a compliance-sensitive environment.

ITEM 3 — Analysis Required: Scope of Exposure
• What Must Be Determined: Was this an isolated incident or part
  of a pattern? The investigation must audit recent Copilot
  prompt histories across the sales team to determine whether
  other proposals were generated using confidential client data
  as reference input. Particular attention must be paid to
  healthcare, financial services, and legal sector accounts,
  where data sensitivity is highest and regulatory stakes are
  most severe.
• Owner: Priya Sharma, Director of AI Governance, in
  coordination with IT Security and Legal.

ITEM 4 — Analysis Required: Platform Data Retention Review
• What Must Be Determined: Does the organization's current
  Microsoft 365 / Copilot configuration retain user-input
  prompt data? If so, for how long, and who has access to it?
  This analysis must confirm whether HealthBridge's confidential
  information is still present in any Copilot session cache,
  SharePoint index, or connected data store — and if so,
  initiate immediate deletion protocols.
• Owner: IT Security team, with findings reported to Priya
  Sharma within 48 hours of incident confirmation.

ITEM 5 — Analysis Required: Contractual and Regulatory Exposure
• What Must Be Determined: Legal must review TechSolutions'
  Master Service Agreement with HealthBridge to assess
  whether a formal data breach notification obligation has been
  triggered. Simultaneously, HIPAA counsel must assess whether
  the input of PHI-adjacent security data into a third-party
  AI platform constitutes a reportable privacy incident under
  45 CFR §164.400–414.
• Owner: General Counsel and external HIPAA compliance advisor.
  Determination must be reached within 72 hours.

ITEM 6 — Escalation Criteria
• Escalation Is Required Immediately If Any of the Following
  Are True:
  — The proposal reached the prospect's inbox at any point,
    even in draft form.
  — The Copilot platform retained or indexed HealthBridge data
    in any shared or cloud-accessible environment.
  — Additional proposals are found to contain cross-client
    data contamination during the scope audit.
  — Legal counsel determines a HIPAA breach notification to
    HealthBridge and/or HHS is required.
• Escalation Path: Jordan Okafor → Evan Rossi (VP of Sales
  Strategy) → General Counsel → CEO. HealthBridge's primary
  contact at TechSolutions (Aisha Mbaye) must be briefed before
  any external notification is made, so the client relationship
  is managed with honesty and care, not reactive damage control.

3C. RESOLUTION & PREVENTION PLAN (120–150 words)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Immediate corrective actions are threefold: quarantine and delete
the compromised proposal from all systems; convene an emergency
briefing with Priya Sharma and Legal to assess notification
obligations; and proactively inform HealthBridge of the incident
before any regulatory timeline requires us to — demonstrating the
transparency our ethical framework demands.

For prevention, three structural controls will be implemented
within 30 days. First, Priya Sharma's governance team will publish
a mandatory Copilot Prompt Policy prohibiting the use of any
identified client data — names, network details, audit findings —
as AI session input. Second, all sales personnel will complete a
60-minute AI data hygiene training module before Copilot access
is restored. Third, proposal templates will be restructured so
that client-specific context fields are populated manually post-
generation, rather than embedded in prompts. These controls
transform a reactive incident response into a proactive governance
upgrade, protecting both clients and TechSolutions from
recurrence.
