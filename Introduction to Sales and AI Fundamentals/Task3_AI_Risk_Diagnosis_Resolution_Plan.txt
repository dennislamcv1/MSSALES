3A. RISK SCENARIO DESCRIPTION (120–150 words)
----------------------------------------------------------------

INCIDENT SUMMARY: COPILOT COMPLIANCE DATA FABRICATION
IN CLIENT PROPOSAL

What Happened:
  During a competitive bid for a mid-sized financial services
  client, a TechSolutions sales associate used Microsoft Copilot
  to draft a technical proposal. Copilot generated a section
  citing specific compliance certification statistics —
  including a claim that TechSolutions held an active FedRAMP
  Moderate authorization — a certification the company was
  pursuing but had not yet received. The associate, under time
  pressure, did not verify the claim before submitting the
  proposal to the client's procurement and legal teams.

Why It Was Problematic:
  The assertion constituted a materially false representation
  during a regulated procurement process. Financial services
  clients are bound by vendor due diligence obligations; an
  inaccurate compliance claim could directly influence a
  contracting decision and expose both the client and
  TechSolutions to regulatory and legal consequences.

Business and Customer Impact:
  Upon discovery during the client's vendor verification process,
  the claim was flagged by the client's compliance officer.
  TechSolutions was temporarily placed on procurement hold,
  damaging a relationship cultivated over eight months. Internal
  credibility was also affected — the incident raised questions
  about the team's proposal governance process and prompted an
  emergency review requested by Evan Rossi, VP of Sales Strategy.


----------------------------------------------------------------
3B. DIAGNOSTIC BREAKDOWN (5–6 items)
----------------------------------------------------------------

ITEM 1: DETECTION METHOD
-------------------------
How the Error Was Found:
  The error was identified externally — by the client's
  compliance officer during vendor credential verification —
  rather than caught internally before submission. A secondary
  internal detection occurred when Aisha Mbaye (Customer Success
  Manager) received an urgent call from the client relationship
  contact and escalated to Jordan Okafor.

Gap Identified:
  TechSolutions had no active pre-submission review gate
  requiring factual verification of AI-generated compliance or
  certification claims. Detection relied entirely on the
  individual associate's judgment, which failed under deadline
  pressure.


ITEM 2: SUSPECTED ROOT CAUSE
------------------------------
Primary Cause:
  Microsoft Copilot hallucinated a plausible but false compliance
  credential, likely drawing on training data referencing
  FedRAMP authorization processes in general and conflating them
  with TechSolutions' own certification status.

Contributing Factors:
  - No internal certification registry was accessible to Copilot
    to cross-check claims against verified TechSolutions data.
  - The associate lacked specific training on AI hallucination
    risk in high-stakes compliance contexts.
  - Time pressure from a compressed bid deadline reduced the
    likelihood of independent verification.


ITEM 3: ANALYSIS REQUIRED
---------------------------
Immediate Analysis:
  (a) Retrieve and audit all Copilot-generated proposals
      submitted in the preceding 90 days to identify any
      additional unverified certification or compliance claims.
  (b) Review the specific Copilot prompt used to generate the
      proposal section to understand what inputs produced the
      fabricated output.
  (c) Cross-reference all active proposals with the official
      TechSolutions certifications register maintained by
      Priya Sharma's governance team.

Broader Analysis:
  Assess whether similar risks exist in other content types —
  including marketing collateral, RFP responses, and client
  security briefings — that also rely on Copilot-generated
  content without a structured review checkpoint.


ITEM 4: ESCALATION CRITERIA
-----------------------------
Escalation Is Required Immediately When:
  - An AI-generated error has been delivered to an external
    client, prospect, or partner (as occurred in this incident).
  - The error involves a regulatory, legal, or contractual
    claim — regardless of whether the client has yet noticed.
  - A client relationship is at risk of formal dispute,
    procurement suspension, or legal action.
  - The error pattern suggests systemic failure across multiple
    proposals rather than a single isolated instance.

Escalation Chain:
  Jordan Okafor → Evan Rossi (VP of Sales Strategy) →
  Priya Sharma (Director of AI Governance) → Legal Counsel
  → CEO notification if client relationship or regulatory
  exposure is confirmed.


ITEM 5: PATTERN RISK ASSESSMENT
---------------------------------
Risk of Recurrence:
  High, given that the team routinely uses Copilot for technical
  proposal drafting under compressed timelines and currently has
  no standardized checkpoint requiring verification of AI-
  generated factual claims prior to external distribution.

Indicators to Monitor:
  - Proposals containing certification names, regulatory
    references, or compliance status language.
  - Proposals generated within 24 hours of a submission
    deadline, where review time is most likely to be compressed.
  - Any Copilot output referencing TechSolutions' own
    qualifications, awards, partnerships, or accreditations.


ITEM 6: STAKEHOLDER COMMUNICATION REQUIREMENT
-----------------------------------------------
Internal:
  The full sales team requires immediate notification of the
  incident type and interim guidance on what categories of
  AI-generated content require mandatory verification before
  submission. Evan Rossi to issue this communication within
  24 hours of incident confirmation.

External (Client):
  A direct acknowledgment must be delivered to the client's
  compliance and procurement contacts by Jordan Okafor and
  Aisha Mbaye, clarifying the accurate certification status,
  explaining how the error occurred, and outlining the
  corrective steps TechSolutions is implementing. The message
  must be honest, concise, and free of defensive language.


----------------------------------------------------------------
3C. RESOLUTION & PREVENTION PLAN (120–150 words)
----------------------------------------------------------------

IMMEDIATE RESOLUTION

The fabricated FedRAMP claim is corrected via a formal written
clarification delivered to the client within 48 hours,
co-signed by Jordan Okafor and Evan Rossi, accompanied by
official documentation of TechSolutions' actual certification
status. The affected associate receives a direct coaching
session on AI verification responsibilities — framed as
learning, not punitive.

SYSTEMIC CORRECTIONS

Priya Sharma implements a Copilot Compliance Content Policy
within two weeks, establishing that any AI-generated reference
to certifications, regulatory status, or legal claims is a
mandatory human-verified field before document release. Luis
Martínez develops a Certified Claims Register — a live, shared
reference document listing all current TechSolutions credentials
— that sales staff must consult before any external submission.

PREVENTION GOING FORWARD

All sales team members complete a 60-minute AI Accuracy and
Verification module within 30 days. A proposal submission
checklist — requiring a named reviewer's sign-off on compliance
content — is embedded into the CRM workflow as a non-bypassable
step. Priya Sharma schedules a quarterly AI error review to
assess whether new Copilot behaviors require policy updates.
Recurrence metrics are reported to Evan Rossi monthly.
